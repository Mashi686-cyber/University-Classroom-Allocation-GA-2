# Research Question Evidence Matrix

| Research Question | Metric | Evidence | Finding | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RQ1**: Is it possible to use Genetic Algorithm in reducing conflicts in classroom allocation? | Total Conflicts (Classroom, Lecturer, Student-Group) | Small/Medium: 100% reduction. Large: 91.4% reduction (from 7 down to a mean of 0.6). | The GA successfully navigates overlapping global constraints that cause conflicts in sequential heuristic allocators. | Large, heavily constrained search spaces can occasionally trap the stochastic search in local optima, leaving minor residual conflicts. |
| **RQ2**: Is it possible to optimize the usage of existing classrooms through Genetic Algorithm? | Classroom Utilization (%) | Medium: +14.1% relative increase. Large: +8.9% relative increase. Small: -0.1% decrease. | The GA generally identifies tighter room-packing configurations than a sequential heuristic, effectively optimizing space. | Hard constraints dominate the fitness hierarchy; the GA will occasionally sacrifice minor utilization (e.g., Small dataset) to resolve severe physical conflicts. |
| **RQ3**: What are the effects of various Genetic Algorithm parameters on classroom allocation? | Final Fitness, Execution Time | Population 50 and Crossover 0.8 achieved rapid convergence (~25 generations) on the Small dataset. Mutation 0.20 disrupted fitness. | Moderate population size and high crossover strike an effective balance for search speed. High mutation is destructive. | Parameters were only evaluated via One-Factor-At-A-Time (OFAT) on the Small dataset; they are not claimed to be globally optimal for complex spaces. |
| **RQ4**: Does the GA approach outperform the simple classroom allocation approach? | Allocated Courses, Total Conflicts, Utilization, Execution Time | The GA allocated identical feasible courses, substantially reduced conflicts (>91%), and generally improved utilization. | The GA provides definitively superior scheduling quality compared to the greedy baseline heuristic. | The primary trade-off is computational execution time, scaling from ~0.3s (Small) to ~1.16s (Large) compared to the baseline's instantaneous (<0.01s) resolution. |
